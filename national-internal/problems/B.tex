\begin{problem}{B}{Breaking the Glass}{2 seconds}

\noindent
Doraemon's 4D pocket has a built-in security grid with $n$ rows and $m$ columns.
Each cell of the grid contains a diagonal piece of glass in the shape of \verb|\| or \verb|/|, which splits that cell into two triangular halves.

\noindent
These diagonal glass pieces divide the interior of the pocket into one or more \textbf{regions}.
A region is a maximal connected open area: informally, any two points in the same region can be joined by a path that does not cross any glass.
Note that the borders of the pocket are \textbf{not} glass, so regions may extend to the edge of the pocket.
The space outside the pocket is not counted as a region.

\noindent
To retrieve a gadget buried deep inside, Doraemon needs to merge all regions into a single connected region.
In one operation, he can break the glass in one cell, removing its diagonal piece entirely.
Determine the minimum number of operations required.

\InputFile

\noindent
First line of input contains two integers $n$ and $m$ ($1 \le n \times m \le 10^6$).\\
The next $n$ lines each contain a string of length $m$ consisting of characters \verb|\| and \verb|/|, representing the pocket's grid.

\OutputFile

\noindent
Output the minimum number of operations required to merge all regions into one big region.

\Examples

\begin{example}
\exmp{1}{
5 6
/\textbackslash//\textbackslash/
\textbackslash/\textbackslash/\textbackslash/
/\textbackslash//\textbackslash/
\textbackslash/\textbackslash/\textbackslash/
\textbackslash/\textbackslash/\textbackslash/
}{
  13
}
\end{example}
\Explaination{\upshape\normalsize The glass diagonals cut the interior of the $5 \times 6$ pocket into many separate regions, as drawn below. Breaking the glass in $13$ suitably chosen cells is enough to connect them all into one region, and no smaller set of breaks works.}

  \draw[gray!40] (0,1)--(6,1) (0,2)--(6,2) (0,3)--(6,3) (0,4)--(6,4);
  % outer border of the pocket
  \draw[very thick] (0,0) rectangle (6,5);

\Explaination{\upshape\normalsize Here the four diagonals meet at the centre and cut the $2 \times 2$ pocket into the four regions shown below.
