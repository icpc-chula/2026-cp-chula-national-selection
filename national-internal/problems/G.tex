\begin{problem}{G}{Gathering}{1 second}

\noindent
You are an adventurer trapped in the wilderness for exactly $K$ days. To survive, you must prevent a dangerous beast from gathering enough energy to attack you. The beast needs $N$ units of energy to launch its attack. Initially, before day $1$, the beast has $0$ energy.

Each day, you must choose exactly one of the following actions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{Gather}: The beast absorbs $t \times X$ units of energy today, where $t$ is the number of consecutive days it has been gathering so far, including today.
  \item \texttt{Disrupt}: You distract the beast and it loses $Y$ units of energy. Its energy cannot become negative, so it becomes exactly $0$ if the loss would make it negative. This action also resets the beast's consecutive gathering streak.
\end{itemize}

You survive the whole expedition if the beast's energy is strictly less than $N$ at the end of every day. Because disrupting the beast costs your own stamina, you want to use this action as rarely as possible.

Find the minimum number of days on which you must choose \texttt{Disrupt}.

\InputFile

\noindent
The input contains a single line with four integers $N$, $X$, $Y$, and $K$.

\OutputFile

\noindent
Print a single integer: the minimum number of \texttt{Disrupt} days required to keep the beast's energy strictly below $N$ for all $K$ days.

\section*{\sffamily{Constraints}}

\begin{itemize}
  \item $1 \le N \le 10^{15}$
  \item $1 \le X \le 10^9$
  \item $1 \le Y \le 10^{15}$
  \item $1 \le K \le 500$
\end{itemize}

\Examples

\begin{example}
\exmp{1}{
10 1 3 4
}{1
}
\Explaination{One possible strategy is to \texttt{Disrupt} on the second day only. Then the beast's energy evolves as follows: day 1: gather $1\cdot 1 = 1$, day 2: disrupt to $\max(0,1-3)=0$, day 3: gather $1\cdot 1 = 1$, day 4: gather $2\cdot 1 = 2$. The energy is always below $10$, so one disruption is enough.}
\end{example}

\begin{example}
\exmp{2}{
100 1 1 1
}{0
}
\Explaination{With $K=1$, the beast can only gather once, so the energy becomes $1$, which is already below $100$. No disruption is needed.}
\end{example}

\end{problem}
