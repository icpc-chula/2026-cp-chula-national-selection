\begin{problem}{C}{Catastrophic Canine Conversion}{X seconds}

\noindent
Nobita stumbles through the front door, knees scraped and eyes brimming with tears.
``Doraemon! The dogs from the vacant lot chased me again --- I had to climb the telephone pole and the nice calico cat I've been feeding ran away when the barking started!''

\noindent
Doraemon freezes mid-dorayaki. Despite being a cat-type robot, he harbours a deep, paralyzing terror of dogs --- the mere sound of barking makes his circuits buzz with static and his round body tremble uncontrollably.
The irony of a cat robot fearing dogs is not lost on him, but phobias transcend logic.
Still, Nobita's despair tugs at his empathy circuits. Summoning every ounce of courage (and finishing his dorayaki for fortification), Doraemon plunges his arm into his 4-Dimensional Pocket and, after rummaging past a Take-copter, a Small Light, and several expired Memory Breads, finally retrieves a sleek silver-and-pink device:

\noindent
\textbf{The Neko-Inu Reversal Ray.}

\noindent
This prototype from the 22nd-century Department of Interspecies Relations fires a shimmering beam that rewrites the quantum species identifier in any animal's DNA: a snarling dog instantly transforms into a purring cat, and a graceful cat morphs into an energetic dog with a single zap.
``With this,'' Doraemon declares, adjusting his bell, ``we can turn every dog in the neighbourhood into a cat! Nobita walks to school safely, the calico returns, and my circuits stop buzzing. Probably.''

\noindent
There is, however, a critical limitation that Doraemon, in his enthusiasm, almost neglects to mention.
The Reversal Ray is an early prototype; its emitter can only sustain a beam for \textbf{one continuous sweep} per trigger pull.
Doraemon must aim at some starting position along the fence, squeeze the trigger, and drag the beam across a \textbf{contiguous block} of animals before releasing.
Every pet caught in the beam's path transforms simultaneously: dogs become cats, cats become dogs --- no exceptions, no selective targeting.
Animals outside the sweep remain unchanged.
Worse still, the ray's energy cartridge holds a finite charge.
Once depleted, the gadget is nothing more than an expensive paperweight.
Doraemon must therefore accomplish the full transformation using as \textbf{few shots as possible}.

\noindent
It so happens that behind Nobita's house, along the riverbank, runs a long wooden fence.
Exactly $n$ pets sit perched there in a neat row, each one either a dog or a cat.
Doraemon, with his robotic vision, scans their arrangement in an instant, encoding it as a binary string where \fonttt{0} represents a dog and \fonttt{1} represents a cat.
Nobita, meanwhile, has drawn up his ideal arrangement on a crumpled sheet of paper --- another binary string of length $n$ --- marking exactly which fence posts should host friendly cats and which, regrettably, must still hold dogs (one cannot simply eliminate all dogs; that would upset the ecological balance, as Doraemon solemnly notes).

\noindent
Formally: you are given two strings $A$ and $B$, each of length $n$, composed of the characters \fonttt{0} (dog) and \fonttt{1} (cat).
In a single operation, you may choose any contiguous substring $A[\ell \ldots r]$ (with $1 \le \ell \le r \le n$) and flip every bit within it --- \fonttt{0} becomes \fonttt{1}, \fonttt{1} becomes \fonttt{0}.
Characters outside the chosen segment stay as they are.
You may perform any number of operations, including zero.
The goal is to make $A$ identical to $B$, minimizing the total number of operations used.

\noindent
Help Doraemon compute this minimum. Nobita's tear-streaked face --- and Doraemon's frazzled circuits --- depend on you.

\InputFile

\noindent
The first line of input contains a single integer $n$ ($1 \le n \le 10^6$), the number of pets on the fence.\\
The second line contains the string $A$ ($|A| = n$), the current arrangement (\fonttt{0} = dog, \fonttt{1} = cat).\\
The third line contains the string $B$ ($|B| = n$), Nobita's desired arrangement.

\OutputFile

\noindent
Output a single integer: the minimum number of Reversal Ray shots required to transform $A$ into $B$.

\Examples

\begin{example}
\exmp{1}{5
11010
00101
}{1
}
\exmp{2}{4
1010
0101
}{1
}

\end{problem}
